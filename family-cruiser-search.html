Our larger family cruiser will be needed from the Anchoring Mission onward. We’re reviewing sleeping space for five, usable standing room at 6′6″, space for the children’s learning, and sailing and anchoring routines for two adults.

The inventory below carries forward our previous research and shortlist. Three initial monohull profiles include public technical references and preliminary family-fit notes. Additional model profiles will grow as their sources, variants, and individual boats are reviewed.

Pacific Seacraft 4040-foot research group · Sourced profileResearched
Builder / designer
Pacific Seacraft · William I. B. Crealock design
Dimensions
LOA with bowsprit 42′2″ · deck length 40′2″ · beam 12′5″ · draft 6′1″ standard / 5′2″ shoal
Rig
Cutter
Keel / rudder
Fin keel and skeg-supported rudder. Confirm ballast construction and the individual boat’s keel variant during inspection.
Tankage
Published references differ. A period builder brochure reports 67 US gal fuel / 122 US gal water. SailboatData reports 70 / 140 US gal. Verify actual tanks and usable capacity.
Layout
Forward double, aft berth, and saloon sleeping positions are shown in the reviewed 1996 broker example. Exact berth arrangement varies by boat.

Family-of-five assessment

Five berths
The fifth sleeping place may use the saloon, affecting the family’s shared living space. Check whether three children can sleep without daily disruption.
6′6″ headroom
The period builder brochure reports 6′6″. That equals Joe’s height with little clearance margin. Measure the galley, saloon, passageways, and shower aboard.
Schoolwork space
The dinette is a candidate work area. Review table space for three learners, book storage, lighting, and how the table works when a saloon berth is in use.
Two-adult sailing and anchoring
Inspect reefing and cutter-sail controls, autopilot, windlass, and access to the bow. Test whether one adult can manage the boat while the other tends to the children.

Next review Measure standing clearance and all five sleeping positions. Confirm tank capacities from the hull records and inspection.

Pacific Seacraft 4440-foot research group · Sourced profileResearched
Builder / designer
Pacific Seacraft · William Crealock design
Dimensions
LOA 44.08 ft · beam 12.67 ft · published maximum draft 6.25 ft
Rig
Cutter
Keel / rudder
Fin keel with rudder on skeg. Verify the selected hull’s draft option and keel construction.
Tankage
Model reference reports 70 US gal fuel / 105 US gal water. Check refits and the individual hull’s usable capacities.
Layout
Builder describes two private staterooms, a main saloon, and a J-shaped galley. The reviewed dealer specification includes convertible dinette sleeping.

Family-of-five assessment

Five berths
Two staterooms require a clear plan for the fifth sleeper and children’s privacy. Verify whether a saloon berth would be permanent or converted daily.
6′6″ headroom
The reviewed dealer listing reports 6.42 ft, approximately 6′5″. This falls below Joe’s 6′6″ standing requirement and needs early onboard assessment.
Schoolwork space
Assess the saloon table and secure storage while accounting for its possible use as a berth. The extra hull length alone does not establish room for three simultaneous workstations.
Two-adult sailing and anchoring
Review cutter controls, sail loads, docking visibility, ground tackle, and windlass operation with two adults. Practice the routine with one adult available to the children.

Next review Resolve the headroom concern before spending time on a particular listing. Then assess berths, access, and refit scope.

Amel Maramu 4640-foot research group · Sourced profileResearched
Builder / designer
Chantiers Amel · Henri Amel / Jacques Carteau design
Dimensions
Published LOA 13.80 m / 45.28 ft · beam 3.99 m / 13.08 ft · draft 1.98 m / 6.50 ft
Rig
Masthead ketch
Keel / rudder
Fin keel with skeg-supported rudder. Inspect structure and steering on the actual boat.
Tankage
Model reference reports 999 L water. Reviewed broker examples report approximately 480–485 L fuel and 999–1,000 L water. Confirm the selected vessel.
Layout
Centre-cockpit arrangement with forward and aft cabins and saloon accommodation. Cabin furniture and sleeping arrangements require hull-specific verification.

Family-of-five assessment

Five berths
Forward and aft cabins give separated spaces. Verify a usable fifth berth and berth lengths, then plan children’s sleeping arrangements and passage access.
6′6″ headroom
Reviewed broker examples report 2.00 m saloon headroom, about 6′6¾″. That leaves a small margin at Joe’s height. Check every working area and overhead fitting.
Schoolwork space
Assess the saloon as a shared work area and check book storage, ventilation, lighting, and whether the table is needed for sleeping.
Two-adult sailing and anchoring
A ketch adds a mizzen and more controls to maintain. Assess the actual furling systems, reefing, autopilot, docking controls, and anchoring routine for the two adults.

Next review Measure headroom and berth lengths. Document the condition of both rigs, furlers, steering, tanks, and existing refit work.

What we’ll check aboard.

Five usable sleeping places

Measure berth lengths and width, plan the children’s sleeping arrangements, and check access at night. Review the effect of saloon sleeping on shared family space.

Standing clearance at 6′6″

Measure the galley, saloon, companionway, cabin passageways, and shower. Note beams, lights, and headliner changes along the places Joe will stand and move.

Space to learn together

Check a practical table arrangement for three children, secure storage for learning materials, lighting, ventilation, and room to work while others cook or rest.

Two adults operating the boat

Review reefing, visibility, autopilot, docking, line handling, and the windlass. Walk through anchoring with one adult at the helm and the other at the bow.

Training continues with the boats we own.

Big Trouble, our 1984 Catalina 36 Mk I, supports seamanship and systems training and can serve on earlier missions when equipped for the passage and crew. Our 1985 O’Day 26 supports sail training and refit practice.
