MISSION 03 · ROUTE OVERVIEW

Bermuda Triangle Rum Run

≈ 3,830 nm22.8–31.9 days underway

From Clearwater, the route rounds the Florida Keys and heads to Bermuda, then sails south to the U.S. Virgin Islands. The return heads west through the Caribbean Sea south of Cuba, rounds its western end through the Yucatán Channel, and crosses the Gulf to Clearwater. St. George’s and St. Thomas are working landfalls.

TEST. LEARN. CORRECT. REPEAT.

Find the weaknesses before the world voyage.

This mission builds familiarity with the vessel and the demands of offshore sailing. The work includes the boat’s systems and the skipper’s routines: communications, watchkeeping, weather routing, maintenance, provisioning, and decision-making.

01 · REVIEW & REPAIR

Apply the Mexico lessons

Review the problems, repairs, and improvements identified during Mission 2. Address maintenance and equipment needs, then test the changes before extending the boat’s range.

02 · PREPARE & PRACTICE

Strengthen offshore routines

Develop and practice communications, watchkeeping, rest, provisioning, maintenance, and emergency procedures. Build familiarity with the boat’s systems and the equipment needed for the planned passages.

03 · PLAN & PROVE

Put preparation to work

Plan the voyage around readiness and appropriate seasonal conditions. Use the offshore passages to evaluate the vessel, equipment, weather decisions, and onboard routines in real conditions.

04 · ASSESS & IMPROVE

Prepare the next step

Document what worked, what failed, and what needs more practice. Turn those findings into repairs, upgrades, and preparation requirements for Mission 4. Completing the voyage does not replace the next readiness review.

MISSION 3 FUNDING

$280,000 cumulative. $50,000 for the next step.

The additional Mission 3 allowance targets ocean-ready systems, offshore safety, and redundancy. It may also address repairs or weaknesses discovered during the Mexico voyage before the Bermuda mission begins.

The Catalina remains the planned challenge boat. This allocation prepares and tests that same vessel for the most demanding mission completed up to this point.

THE MISSION LOG

Bring home the lessons.

This page will grow with repairs, equipment updates, training notes, route planning, photos, and offshore reports. The work and costs will be documented as they happen.

After the Rum Run, the mission report will cover the actual route, distance, duration, costs, repairs, and lessons learned. Because this is the strongest pre-circumnavigation test, those findings feed directly into Mission 4, Phase 1: corrective work, additional training, shakedown, earning my USCG OUPV “six-pack” captain’s license, and final readiness aboard the same vessel.
