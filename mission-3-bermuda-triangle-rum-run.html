PROVE THE BOAT OFFSHORE

The rehearsal before the long way home.

Mission 2 introduces the actual expedition vessel. Mission 3 takes that same boat into the next stage of offshore preparation, building on what the Mexico voyage reveals. There is no second boat purchase in this mission.

I’ll use the Bermuda Triangle Rum Run to expose weaknesses in the boat, equipment, procedures, and my own preparation. The goal is to find what needs work, correct it, and carry those lessons into the circumnavigation. The name brings the personality; the offshore rehearsal gives the mission its purpose.

THE BOAT

The same expedition vessel

The boat acquired and commissioned in Mission 2, with repairs and improvements informed by the Mexico proving voyage.

THE MILESTONE

$1,150,000 cumulative net

An additional $150,000 beyond Mission 2’s milestone, planned for offshore preparation, the voyage, and contingency.

THE PLAN

An offshore proving mission

The Bermuda Triangle Rum Run. Specific ports, passage legs, timing, and seasonal weather windows will be developed during preparation.

TEST. LEARN. CORRECT. REPEAT.

Find the weaknesses before the world voyage.

This mission builds familiarity with the vessel and the demands of offshore sailing. The work includes the boat’s systems and the skipper’s routines: communications, watchkeeping, weather routing, maintenance, provisioning, and decision-making.

01 · REVIEW & REPAIR

Apply the Mexico lessons

Review the problems, repairs, and improvements identified during Mission 2. Address maintenance and equipment needs, then test the changes before extending the boat’s range.

02 · PREPARE & PRACTICE

Strengthen offshore routines

Develop and practice communications, watchkeeping, rest, provisioning, maintenance, and emergency procedures. Build familiarity with the boat’s systems and the equipment needed for the planned passages.

03 · PLAN & PROVE

Put preparation to work

Plan the voyage around readiness and appropriate seasonal conditions. Use the offshore passages to evaluate the vessel, equipment, weather decisions, and onboard routines in real conditions.

04 · ASSESS & IMPROVE

Prepare the next step

Document what worked, what failed, and what needs more practice. Turn those findings into repairs, upgrades, and preparation requirements for Mission 4. Completing the voyage does not replace the next readiness review.

MISSION 3 FUNDING

$1,150,000 cumulative. $150,000 for the next step.

The additional Mission 3 allocation covers repairs discovered during the Mexico voyage, upgrades, maintenance, equipment, sails and rigging, spares, provisions, fuel and operating costs, port and clearance expenses, and contingency.

The expedition vessel is already acquired in Mission 2. This allocation prepares and operates that same boat for the offshore rehearsal.

These are conservative planning estimates, not a claim that every dollar will be required. Actual costs may be lower. The vessel’s condition after Mission 2, confirmed equipment needs, and the final passage plan will make the budget more precise.

The campaign’s verified net total accounts for processing fees, refunds, dispute adjustments, and the provisional 25% reserve. The reserve is a planning allowance, not a final tax calculation. View live campaign progress.

Projected costs and actual spending will be identified separately as details become available. An itemized Mission 3 budget has not yet been published.

MONEY UNLOCKS THE MISSION

Readiness gives the green light.

Reaching the milestone funds the mission. Departure still depends on skipper competence, a sound vessel, completed preparation work, tested safety and communications equipment, financial reserves, and a suitable weather window.

Passage planning will include weather routing, provisioning, crew rest, port requirements, contingency options, and abort and recovery plans. The aim is to build offshore experience through disciplined preparation and decisions.

Current published plan: the offshore proving objectives and funding milestone are outlined here. A departure date, final route, and completed readiness checks have not yet been announced.
