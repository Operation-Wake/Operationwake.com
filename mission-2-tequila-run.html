ACQUIRE & COMMISSION

The boat for the long way home.

Mission 1 builds experience aboard Training Sails. Mission 2 puts that experience toward selecting, acquiring, refitting, and commissioning the actual expedition vessel—the boat intended for the offshore proving mission and eventual circumnavigation.

I’ll use the Mexico voyage to begin learning that boat in real conditions: how she handles, how her systems work together, what needs attention, and which procedures need more practice. The Tequila Run gives the mission its name. Getting to know the vessel gives it its purpose.

THE BOAT

The expedition vessel

The boat acquired here is intended to carry through Missions 3 and 4. A specific vessel has not yet been announced.

THE MILESTONE

$1,000,000 cumulative net

An additional $500,000 beyond Mission 1’s milestone, planned for acquisition, commissioning, and the Mexico proving voyage.

THE PLAN

The Mexico proving voyage

Begin learning the expedition boat at sea. Specific ports, passage legs, timing, and weather windows will be developed during preparation.

BUY THE BOAT. BUILD THE FAMILIARITY.

A purchase is only the beginning.

Lessons from Training Sails and the Texas run will inform the selection and preparation of the expedition vessel. Each stage is a chance to identify weaknesses and address them before increasing the difficulty.

01 · SELECT & ASSESS

Find the right vessel

Evaluate candidate boats against the intended expedition, including predominantly solo sailing, maintainability, and offshore preparation needs. Survey findings, condition, and realistic refit costs will inform the purchase decision.

02 · REFIT & EQUIP

Prepare for offshore work

Address deferred maintenance and the work identified during assessment. Plan safety, navigation, communications, sails, rigging, and system upgrades around the actual vessel. Equipment choices and the detailed work list will be shared as they are confirmed.

03 · COMMISSION & PRACTICE

Learn this boat

Test systems and practice aboard the vessel before the extended voyage. Build familiarity with handling, maintenance, emergency procedures, and onboard routines. Record problems, make corrections, and repeat the checks.

04 · VOYAGE & REVIEW

Take the lessons to sea

Use the Mexico voyage as the initial proving trip. Document how the boat, equipment, and procedures perform in real conditions. Repairs, upgrades, and lessons identified here feed directly into Mission 3’s offshore preparation.

MISSION 2 FUNDING

$1,000,000 cumulative. $500,000 for the next step.

The additional Mission 2 allocation covers vessel acquisition, survey and acquisition expenses, taxes, documentation and closing costs, initial offshore refit, deferred maintenance, safety equipment, navigation and communications, sails and rigging, spares, provisions, operating costs, and contingency.

The working vessel purchase range is approximately $275,000–$325,000. That leaves room within the allocation to prepare and operate the boat. The purchase price is one part of getting the vessel ready for the expedition.

These are conservative planning estimates, not a claim that every dollar will be required. Actual costs may be lower. Candidate boats, negotiated prices, survey results, and confirmed refit requirements will make the budget more precise.

The campaign’s verified net total accounts for processing fees, refunds, dispute adjustments, and the provisional 25% reserve. The reserve is a planning allowance, not a final tax calculation. View live campaign progress.

Projected costs and actual spending will be identified separately as details become available. A vessel-specific itemized budget has not yet been published.

MONEY UNLOCKS THE MISSION

Readiness gives the green light.

FinancialCompetenceOperationalVesselExpeditionPassage

Reaching the milestone funds the mission. Departure still depends on skipper competence, required refit work, tested safety and communications equipment, financial reserves, passage planning, and a suitable weather window.

Route preparation will include port and clearance requirements, provisioning, contingency options, and abort and recovery planning. The final voyage must fit the readiness of both the vessel and the skipper.

Current published plan: the acquisition range and preparation objectives are outlined here. A selected vessel, departure date, final route, and completed readiness checks have not yet been announced.
