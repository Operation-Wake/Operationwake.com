THE EXPEDITION WE’VE BEEN BUILDING TOWARD

One world. One long way home.

Mission 4 is a roughly 25,000-mile, predominantly solo westbound circumnavigation from Florida back to Florida. I’ll sail the same expedition vessel acquired in Mission 2 and proven offshore in Mission 3, carrying forward the preparation and lessons of every mission before it.

This is the goal behind the progression: pursue something extraordinary through faith, perseverance, preparation, and hard work. I want the journey to show others facing their own challenges that meaningful goals are still worth pursuing, one prepared step at a time.

FLORIDA TO FLORIDA · WESTBOUND

Start at home. Go around. Come home.

The working route begins in Florida, transits the Panama Canal, heads north along the Pacific side to Cabo San Lucas, and then turns west. It continues across the Pacific and South Pacific, through Australia and/or an appropriate Indian Ocean route, around South Africa, and across the Atlantic to Florida.

01 · FLORIDA → PANAMA → CABO

Reach the Pacific

Depart Florida, transit the Panama Canal, and work north to Cabo San Lucas before turning west. Exact passage legs and stops will be developed during route planning.

02 · PACIFIC & SOUTH PACIFIC

Keep heading west

Plan the Pacific crossing and onward passages around seasonal weather, provisioning, maintenance, and rest. Hawaii is not part of the current intended route.

03 · INDIAN OCEAN → SOUTH AFRICA

Follow the suitable route

Develop the onward route through Australia and/or an appropriate Indian Ocean passage toward South Africa. Routing and timing will depend on conditions and expedition readiness.

04 · ATLANTIC → FLORIDA

Bring it home

Complete the westbound circuit across the Atlantic and return to Florida. The final passage plan will define the necessary stops, weather windows, and contingencies.

This is a route concept, not a finalized passage plan. Specific ports, dates, distances, and seasonal gates will be refined before departure. Progress will account for fuel and water, provisions, repairs, customs and clearance, educational stops, and crew rest.

THREE MISSIONS OF LESSONS

Preparation comes with us.

The Texas run develops practical experience. The Mexico run introduces the expedition vessel. The Bermuda Triangle run tests the boat and skipper offshore. Mission 4 begins with reviewing those lessons and completing the work they identify.

Preparation will cover vessel condition, required repairs and upgrades, sails and rigging, safety and communications, spares, maintenance routines, provisioning, weather routing, rest, and emergency procedures. Detailed requirements will follow the actual boat and the experience gained aboard her.

Predominantly solo sailing makes familiarity with the boat and disciplined routines central to the plan. The preparation must support both the vessel and the person making the decisions aboard her.

MISSION 4 FUNDING

Build the budget before setting the target.

The final funding milestone remains to be determined. It will be developed from the detailed route, the condition of the vessel after Mission 3, and the preparation and operating needs of the circumnavigation.

Budget planning will account for remaining refit and maintenance, equipment and spares, provisions, fuel and water, transit and port expenses, communications, repairs, reserves, and contingencies. The vessel itself is acquired in Mission 2.

Published estimates will use conservative high-side planning assumptions. Actual costs may be lower. We’ll refine the budget as real information replaces assumptions and distinguish projected costs from actual spending.

The main campaign’s progress bar currently covers the published cumulative milestones for Missions 1–3. It does not represent a final circumnavigation target. View live campaign progress.

COMMUNITY OBJECTIVES

Help shape the experiences along the way.

During Mission 4, the community may help choose among crew-approved safe options along the route. That could mean a landmark photo, a maritime museum, a local food, a conversation with a local sailor, or an educational objective.

The crew will first define acceptable routes, destinations, seasons, weather gates, required stops, and maximum diversions. Community participation will work within those boundaries. Safety and fundamental expedition decisions stay with the crew.

Participation details will be announced before the expedition. There is no open ballot or voting-ticket system, and contributors do not vote on whether a funded mission goes ahead.
