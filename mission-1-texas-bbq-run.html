THE FIRST EXTENDED FIELD EXERCISE

A serious proving trip. A very good excuse.

The Texas BBQ Run is the first extended real-world training mission in The Long Way Home Challenge. I’ll rebuild Training Sails, our existing O’Day 26, and use the preparation and voyage to turn sailing fundamentals into experience.

The barbecue gives the trip its name. The real objective is to practice planning, navigation, boat handling, maintenance, and decision-making—and come home with a clearer understanding of what I need to improve before the next mission.

BUILD THE SAILOR AND THE BOAT

Earn the departure.

The preparation follows the same principle I carry into the larger challenge: learn the fundamentals, practice repeatedly, find weaknesses, correct them, and then increase the difficulty.

01 · LEARN & PRACTICE

Build competence

Formal sailing education and repeated practice aboard Training Sails will build the foundation. Preparation will include boat handling, navigation, weather awareness, emergency procedures, and knowing when to stop or change the plan.

02 · REBUILD & EQUIP

Prepare Training Sails

Assess the boat, establish the refit scope, and address the work needed for the voyage. Safety, navigation, communications, and reliable onboard systems come before departure. The detailed work list and equipment choices will be shared as they are confirmed.

03 · TEST & CORRECT

Prove the preparation

Use local practice and shakedown trips to test the boat, equipment, and procedures. Record problems, make corrections, and repeat the checks before taking on the extended voyage.

04 · PLAN & REVIEW

Prepare for the unexpected

Develop passage plans, provisioning, reserves, communications, and contingency options. Weather windows, safe stopping points, and abort and recovery plans will shape the final route.

MISSION 1 FUNDING

$500,000 to start the journey.

This cumulative net milestone covers clearing the remaining debt so it does not burden the family during the challenges, rebuilding Training Sails, training, safety equipment, provisions, operating expenses, repairs, and contingency.

Our family is already cutting unnecessary expenses, taking additional work where possible, and directing freed-up money toward the remaining obligations. The campaign works alongside that effort.

These are conservative planning estimates, not a claim that every dollar will be required. Actual costs may be lower. Refit assessments, equipment decisions, and real operating experience will make the budget more precise.

The campaign’s verified net total accounts for processing fees, refunds, dispute adjustments, and the provisional 25% reserve. The reserve is a planning allowance, not a final tax calculation. View live campaign progress.

Projected costs and actual spending will be identified separately as details become available. A detailed itemized Mission 1 budget has not yet been published.
